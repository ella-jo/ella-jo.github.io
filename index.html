Projects

Clickable Cards

functional x accessible x flexible

I believe a key component to a successful modern web UI is a clickable surface. A surface that is able to not only serve as a portal moving a user forward through their journey, but to act as a "heads-up" of what is ahead.

At WEB, we had never had a successful clickable card, at least not implemented in an accessible way. When I saw this gap in our code library, I decided to do some research and develop one. I also worked closely with our accessibility team to make sure that this solution was accessible to those using screen readers. You can see the original application of the clickable card on Redbird Well.

From there, I developed other iterations of the clickable card in tandem with our designer. These included more aesthetic elements, like background images, gradients, and animations while maintaining the functionality and accessible nature of the base card. You can see these iterations in action on Research and Innovation.

Icon Library

sustainable x comprehensive x consistent

WEB originally had its own custom icon library. Cool right? But it was very limited and build upon (creating an updated font file, then adding custom classes in css), creating a cycle where we would create icons but never add them or use completely new, inconsistent ones. Sizing and style would be totally off.

We purchased a new, larger set, but we found that lacking too, and we never officially implemented it into our css like we had done with our previous set. We didn't have an interface that made it easy to view and choose icons.

I had made it my mission to build or find us an alternative. Building seemed to only perpetuate our issues, so I just kept searching on how to source a comprehensive, consistent icon set. And that's when I found Google's Material Symbols.

Not only are Material Symbols free to use under the Apache License Version 2.0, but they are consistent, plentiful, and easy to view with Google's library. They cover all things from utilities to abstract ideas.

Using the guide, I was able to implement the library into our own design system. I preserved their method of using ligatures to pull the icons, and even applied it to pulling two additional sets of icons we needed for branding and filetypes (that Google does not provide). I worked with our accessibility team to assure that using ligatures was a good way to move forward and made adjustments as needed.

We now have a system that is forward-thinking and backwards-compatible.

Web Articles

creative x narrative x collaborative

I have had the opportunity to work with University Marketing on designing and developing online versions of STATE alumni magazine articles. These serve as a method to provide more content and interactivity than the written pieces.

These projects start with a discussion with the writer, a look at the design of the print piece, and access to photos and documents of additional content they'd like included. From there, they let me get to work. After review of the draft and any updates to be made, the custom HTML and CSS is posted live to our News site (Wordpress) alongside the printed article. These articles are submitted to the CASE Circle of Excellence competition each year, and two have won awards.

These are some of the most creatively fulfilling projects I have ever done.
